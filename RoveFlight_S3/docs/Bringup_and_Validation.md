# Engineering bring-up and qualification

Work with propellers removed, JP1 removed and a current-limited supply first. This board has no demonstrated free-flight capability. Do not connect motors until the supply and interlock checks below pass.

1. Inspect polarity and pin-1 orientation of the module, MOSFETs, diodes, shunt, polymer capacitors, sensors and USB connector. Check resistance between battery, VM, 3.3 V and ground. Compare physical pin numbers with Connector_Pinouts.csv rather than cable colors.
2. Apply 3.7 V through an initially low current limit; measure protected battery, VM, SYS_IN, 3.3 V and sensor 3.3 V. Increase the limit only after excluding shorts. Verify correct and reverse input polarity behavior with a deliberately limited source. USB-only power must leave VM unpowered.
3. Enumerate native USB and flash the disarmed bring-up skeleton. Check EN/reset and BOOT recovery. Confirm all four gate pads remain low throughout power-on, reset, USB insertion/removal and programming. Confirm MCU-side PWM toggling cannot reach a motor gate with JP1 removed.
4. Exercise U6 latch with a controlled test harness and no motors: establish healthy heartbeat, confirm SAFE_CLR_N high, fit JP1 and issue a single ARM_CLK edge. Then test SW3, USB insertion, EN reset, deliberate heartbeat freeze, forced-low/forced-high heartbeat and VM undervoltage. Each detected clear must latch disabled; recovery must not rearm until a new edge. Scope actual watchdog timeout and gate discharge time. Inspect internal software-reset behavior separately from EN reset.
5. Verify BMI270 chip ID and configuration upload, gyro/data-ready/FIFO timing, static accelerometer axes and signed rotations. Verify BMP390 pressure/temperature and SPI chip select independence. Test ToF identity/range/interrupt/XSHUT with the required I/O setting and eventual enclosure. Test the IR receiver with a supported 38-kHz protocol.
6. Calibrate battery ADC over the full operating range against a meter. Calibrate current sense using known DC loads and a reference ammeter, including offset, zero, 1 A and 4 A. Inspect shunt Kelvin routing and PWM aliasing. Verify receiver UART levels, packet age checks and behavior when disconnected.
7. Use resistive test loads before motors. Scope 3.3 V at the module supply pads, sensor supply, VM, drain and gate with short probe grounds during wireless TX bursts and independent/simultaneous load steps. Check minimum ESP rail, regulator startup/recovery, diode temperatures and whether ground bounce corrupts SPI/IMU readings. VM must stay within capacitor/regulator ratings under switching, battery disconnect and back-driven motors. Do not assume the flyback network clamps every battery-disconnect transient.
8. Connect one motor without a propeller. Confirm numbering, direction and off behavior; then repeat for all four. Measure running, startup and brief locked-rotor current using an appropriately rated current-limited fixture. Reject a motor/propeller combination outside the documented envelope. Characterize MOSFET, flyback diode, reverse MOSFET, fuse, shunt, regulator and copper temperatures at worst battery and duty cycles; flyback heating can be worse at partial duty.
9. Use a restrained thrust fixture before any propeller-loaded aircraft test. Verify mixer signs, command-loss policy, sensor fault detection, watchdog fault and manual kill. Measure vibration spectra and IMU clipping; tune filtering and PID only after mechanical and electrical issues are corrected. Repeat RF-range testing with motors, battery and enclosure installed.

Record voltages, waveforms, current limits, ambient temperature, component temperatures, exact motor/propeller/battery models, firmware revision and outcomes. No load rating, flight readiness, EMC/ESD qualification or certification is established by ERC/DRC alone.
